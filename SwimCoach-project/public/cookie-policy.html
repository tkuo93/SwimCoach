1. What Are Cookies

Cookies are small text files stored on your device (computer, phone, tablet) when you visit a website. They help websites remember your preferences, keep you logged in, and understand how you use the service.

Cookies can be "session" (deleted when you close your browser) or "persistent" (remain until they expire or you delete them). They can be "first-party" (set by the site you're visiting) or "third-party" (set by other domains).

2. How We Use Cookies

SwimCoach uses cookies for the following purposes:

  • Authentication (Essential): Session cookies keep you logged in as you navigate the app. Without these, you'd need to sign in on every page.
  • Security (Essential): CSRF protection and rate limiting.
  • Preferences (Functional): Remembering your selected LLM model, UI preferences.
  • Analytics (Optional): Understanding usage patterns (only with your consent via the onboarding acknowledgment).
Note: We do not use advertising cookies, tracking pixels, or third-party marketing cookies. We do not sell your data to advertisers.

5. Managing Cookies

5.1 Browser Controls

You can control cookies through your browser settings:

  • Chrome: Settings → Privacy and security → Cookies and other site data
  • Firefox: Settings → Privacy & Security → Cookies and Site Data
  • Safari: Preferences → Privacy → Cookies and website data
  • Edge: Settings → Cookies and site permissions → Manage and delete cookies

Blocking essential cookies (connect.sid) will prevent you from staying logged in.

5.2 localStorage Data

To clear SwimCoach's localStorage data (profiles, workouts, preferences):

  • Use the "Clear All Data" button in Settings → Debug (if enabled)
  • Or: DevTools → Application → Local Storage → https://swimcoach.app → Delete all
  • Or: Browser "Clear browsing data" → select "Cookies and other site data"

5.3 Opt-Out of Analytics

Analytics tracking only occurs after you acknowledge the onboarding privacy modal. To opt out later, clear sc_session_id and sc_privacy_ack_* from localStorage.
